01 / Scenario

A maintenance operator remotely controls a robotic arm to position a sensor at a designated attachment location on an equipment surface.

Innovation

The pointing task becomes sensor-attachment positioning: the circular target is the acceptable region for the sensor center. A fixed-link robotic arm and balanced opposite-angle movements connect the task to remote manipulation.

Application and design rationale

This design represents the speed–accuracy tradeoff in remote sensor installation. It investigates how travel distance and positioning tolerance affect operation time, helping inform the design of accurate, efficient equipment-maintenance interfaces. Results describe this simulation rather than physical robot performance.

Custom formula (reported session)

MT = 270.73 + 180.76 × log₂(A / W + 1) (ms)
R² = 0.8303

Participant-reported results from a completed session: a = 270.73 ms and b = 180.76 ms/bit. This reference model explains 83.03% of the variation in the condition means used for that fit. New sessions calculate their own model in Results.

The base sits at the center of the equipment’s lower edge. Two rigid 245 px links rotate at their joints. This is a position-control simulation, without robot dynamics or collision constraints.

ID = log₂(A / W + 1)

Movement distance (A): 150, 250, 350, 450, 550 px.
Target width (W): 40, 60, 80, 100 px.
Index of difficulty (ID) is measured in bits.

20 conditions × 6 repetitions = 120 trials. Each block contains all 20 conditions in random order. Movement alternates rightward and leftward (60 trials each), with upward and downward components. Each condition includes three pairs of opposite directions: 3 rightward and 3 leftward trials. Angles vary across the available workspace; only angles that preserve the specified distance and keep both endpoints visible are used. Directions are pooled in the analysis. Sensor diameter stays at 24 px, and mouse-to-sensor gain is 1:1. Distances use CSS pixels. The workspace fills the available width and height without scaling the specified distances. Link lengths are set for this layout and stay fixed while dragging.

02 / Instructions

  1. Use a desktop mouse. Keep the window size, browser zoom and input device unchanged throughout the experiment.
  2. Click Start experiment once. Press and hold the left mouse button on the blue sensor to start measuring movement time (MT).
  3. Drag the sensor and release when its center is inside the circular target. The whole sensor does not need to fit.
  4. If you miss, grab the sensor again and correct its position. Timing continues through corrections.
  5. After success, the next trial appears automatically. The sensor and target switch sides and may appear at different heights. Find the sensor at its new starting position and grab it when ready. This return movement and waiting time are excluded from the next trial’s measurement.
  6. After 120 successful trials, the Fitts’ Law chart opens automatically. It shows one Mouse series, a regression line, the complete Shannon equation and R². Use View Fitts’ Law chart to reopen it after closing. You can download the chart or export individual trials and condition means as CSV. To begin a new session, click Restart experiment below the workspace; export any results you want to keep before confirming the reset.

The sensor stops at the workspace boundary if the mouse leaves it; timing continues so you can drag back and correct it. Switching tabs, losing window focus, or resizing interrupts an active trial. Restore the original window size before retrying after a resize. Use Retry to repeat that condition. Interrupted attempts do not count toward the 120 trials or condition means. Scrolling this information column does not move the workspace.

Data stays in this page’s memory. Export before reloading or closing. Partial exports are available; the count column identifies incomplete conditions. Start screen recording before the experiment if required for your assignment.
